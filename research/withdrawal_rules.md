# Withdrawal rules: the fixed withdrawal against flexible rules

Compares the fixed CPI-indexed withdrawal with rules that can cut income after a bad year, per portfolio, rule and withdrawal rate. A comparison only: no pass/fail rule and no search trial; it selects nothing and recommends nothing. Spec: docs/methodology/withdrawal-rules.md.

- data: up to 2018-12-31 only (validation and holdout stay sealed)
- withdrawal_rules.yaml sha256: `599bba2602e4340388c0d14e8ddff2bb2d2a45d24ed6efbd9227d68fef70c752`
- decision.yaml sha256: `88ab8b08a34f8b3d106fd7670af02e3e827b86467a2755eb3620ada4490aa76d` (read unchanged)
- longrun.yaml sha256: `ebd3d59afd1bfbc79118747a944f74a8b3c62688aeddfe2e201183e10acfe061` (read unchanged)
- historical view: start years 2005 to 2014, each to 2018-12-31
- long run: 10000 paths of 30 years, 12-month blocks drawn from 168 months (2005-01 to 2018-12, circular), seed 20261008; the same paths as the long-run report
- trading costs: 5 bps per unit traded
- 8 portfolios x 5 rules x 4 withdrawal rates = 160 rows per view
- portfolios: A (current: QQQ/GLD 200d trend); B (SPY/GLD 200d trend); C (SPY/IEF 200d trend); D (static 60/40 SPY/IEF); E (static SPY 60 / IEF 20 / GLD 20); F (switched mix, SPY 200d signal); G (static 25% each SPY/TLT/GLD/SHY); CASH (100% SHY)

## Rules

Every rule starts at rate x starting value / 12 a month and changes the amount once a year: on the first trading day of each January in the historical view, and every 12 months (months 13, 25, ...) in the long run, where resampled paths start in any calendar month. The first year has no change. All parameters are the same at every rate, and a rule uses only values known at the decision point.

- fixed: last year's monthly amount raised by CPI each year; the rule every other report uses.
- skip_raise_after_loss: as fixed, but no CPI raise after a year in which the portfolio's own return (before withdrawals) was negative.
- guardrails: take the fixed amount; if 12 x amount / value is above 120.0% of the rate, cut it by 10.0%; if below 80.0% of the rate, raise it by 10.0%. Guyton-Klinger's capital preservation and prosperity rules with their published bands and steps, with one deviation: no 15-year exception (the original stops the cuts late in life). Their inflation rule is replaced by the fixed rule's CPI step.
- pct_of_balance: each year 12 x amount = rate x value.
- floor_ceiling: rate x value / 12, kept within -2.5% and +5.0% of last year's amount after CPI (Vanguard's published bands, not fitted).

## Notes

- This is a comparison. It has no pass/fail rule, adds no trials to any search run, selects nothing and recommends nothing.
- The rules are published or textbook rules with fixed parameters; the bands are the published ones, not fitted to these data.
- The long-run decision point is every 12 months, not January.
- Income is real: each year's payments in starting-date money (historical: deflated by the CPI usable on 1 January of that year; long run: by the path's own inflation to the start of that 12-month period), as a percentage of the starting income (rate x starting value). A path that ran out counts 0 for every later year and, in the year it ran out, what it received.
- Years below a line: historical, the share of all calendar years of all start years (pooled), a year in or after running out counting as below every line; long run, the share of paths whose real income is below the line in any year, paths that ran out counting as below.
- The historical view has 10 overlapping start years, so it is 10 related paths, not 10 independent tests. Its worst start year is the one with the lowest real value at the end.
- Each month the withdrawal is taken first, then the day's (or month's) return. It is taken proportionally from all holdings at no extra cost, as in the decision helper. A path is empty once its value is at or below one billionth of the start; in a month emptied by the withdrawal the income is what was left.
- Typical is the median path; bad case (worst 5%) is the value 1 path in 20 is below. Lowest value is the lowest nominal value, as a percentage of the starting value.
